SECOND MEASUREMENT SM-019 · SUB-AWARD LOOKUP

Did this grant recipient report its sub-awards on time?

When a state agency, university, city or company that holds a federal grant passes money on to someone else (a sub-award of $30,000 or more; $25,000 before November 2020), it must report it by the end of the following month. Type a recipient’s name, for example your state’s education or health department, to see how many of its sub-awards since October 2017 were reported late. Data from USAspending.gov; the paper and method are at sm-019.html.

A sub-award is late when its first report is in a month after the month following the sub-award, the deadline in 2 CFR 170, appendix A. Months are counted from the sub-award’s action date to the month the report was published. Late dollars leave out sub-awards reported as larger than the prime award they came from, as the paper does. A recipient appears under the unique entity ID on its reports; one with several IDs appears more than once. The fiscal year runs October to September. A late report can have a reason the data does not show. Data: lookup.json, built by build_lookup.py from the paper’s exhibit file.
