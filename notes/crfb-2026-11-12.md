# Maya MacGuineas (CRFB) on the first month of fiscal 2027 (9 Nov 2026): recompute, 12 Nov 2026

This supports the statement. Every number in it holds against Treasury's own data. One holds only on the usual budget convention: debt held by the public is above a year of GDP measured over the fiscal year, but below the economy's latest quarterly pace.

Our path: Treasury's Fiscal Data API (Monthly Treasury Statement tables 1 and 9; Debt to the Penny), read 12 November 2026; BEA's nominal GDP via FRED (series GDP); the 2026 Social Security Trustees Report.

| Claim in the statement | Ours |
|---|---|
| "we have already borrowed $409 billion" in October (CBO's estimate) | $404.1 billion, Treasury's actual deficit for October 2026 (receipts $368.9B, outlays $773.0B). CRFB published the actual itself on 12 Nov |
| "the gross national debt reaching a whopping $40 trillion" | First above $40 trillion on 18 August 2026 ($40.047T); $40.584T on 10 November |
| "debt held by the public surpassing our entire economy" | $32.425T on 30 September 2026: 100.6% of fiscal-year 2026 GDP ($32.235T, the average of four quarters); 98.2% of the latest quarter's annual rate ($33.010T, Q3 2026) |
| "interest exceeding defense spending" | Fiscal 2026: net interest $1,051.8B, national defense $965.2B, interest higher by $86.6B. Also true in fiscal 2025 ($970.4B vs $916.6B) |
| "Social Security is approaching insolvency in less than six years" | The retirement (OASI) trust fund is projected to run out in the fourth quarter of 2032, about six years from now; the combined retirement and disability funds in the third quarter of 2034 |

- The ratio of debt to GDP depends on the denominator. CBO and CRFB divide by GDP over the fiscal year, which gives 100.6%. The economy grew through the year, so its pace in the latest quarter is higher, and against that the debt is 98.2%.
- "Insolvency" here means the trust fund runs out, not that benefits stop: the Trustees project 78% of scheduled retirement benefits payable after 2032.
